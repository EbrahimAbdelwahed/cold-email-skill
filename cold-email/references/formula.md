# The Formula, Checklist, and Tracking

## Why most cold emails fail

The typical approach: collect 100 addresses, copy one template, swap the name, attach a CV, send, wait. When nothing comes back, the conclusion is "cold email doesn't work". Usually the message is the problem: it never says why this recipient was chosen, what the sender can contribute, or why the recipient should spend time on it.

## Context + Relevance + Proof + A Small Ask

Use it as a guide, not a rigid script.

| Part | Question it answers | Good example | Bad example |
|------|--------------------|--------------|-------------|
| **Context** | Why are you contacting *this* person? | "I saw your team just shipped offline sync in the mobile app." | "I came across your esteemed organization." |
| **Relevance** | Why are you a potential fit for *their* work? | "I've spent the last year building React Native apps with local-first data." | A list of every technology you know. |
| **Proof** | What can they look at? | "Here's a deployed app I built with conflict-free sync: [link]" | "I'm a passionate, hard-working developer." |
| **Small ask** | What's the one easy next step? | "Are you open to bringing on a developer right now?" | "Please let me know about any openings, and could you also refer me and mentor me?" |

## Proof of work

A good proof link should quickly answer:
1. What did you build?
2. What problem does it solve?
3. What did *you* personally contribute?
4. Can I see it working?
5. What evidence shows the result?

Pick the 1–2 examples most relevant to the recipient (backend role → API/DB/systems work; frontend role → a polished deployed app; freelance client → work that demonstrates the exact service offered).

If the user has no internship or clients yet: a deployed useful project, a documented solution to a real problem, or a meaningful open-source contribution all count. Never inflate contribution or claim unsupported results.

## Quality checklist

Run before showing the draft to the user. Every item must pass.

- [ ] Recipient is the right person for this goal (founder / recruiter / hiring manager / employee / business owner)
- [ ] First sentence contains a specific, genuine, verifiable observation about the recipient
- [ ] Relevance is tailored to this recipient — not a generic skill list
- [ ] 1–2 concrete proof items, each with a working link
- [ ] Exactly one small, easy-to-answer ask
- [ ] 80–150 words (email); shorter for DMs and follow-ups
- [ ] Subject line is specific (email only)
- [ ] No clichés ("esteemed organization", "Dear Sir", "I hope this email finds you well", "kindly revert")
- [ ] No exaggeration, no invented metrics, no fake personal connection
- [ ] Reads like a human wrote it — plain words, short paragraphs
- [ ] Would still make sense if the recipient read only the first two lines

## Follow-up timing

- First follow-up: 3–5 business days after the original.
- Optional final follow-up: about a week later.
- Then move on. Stop immediately if asked to.

## Handling replies

- Asked for a resume → send it promptly.
- Asked about experience → answer directly, with the single most relevant example.
- Offered a call → propose two or three concrete time slots, and prepare what to discuss.
- "Nothing right now" → thank them and leave the door open.

## Tracking

Suggested columns for a tracker (CSV / spreadsheet / markdown table):

`date | person | role | company | channel | goal | reason for reaching out | message version | follow-up date | response | outcome`

Metrics (keep the denominator consistent — delivered messages):
- **Reply rate** = replies / delivered
- **Positive reply rate** = interested or constructive replies / delivered
- **Outcome rate** = calls, interviews, or deals / delivered

Don't confuse impressions with replies, or replies with offers. Look for patterns over time: right people? shorter messages better? which proof links lead to conversations?
